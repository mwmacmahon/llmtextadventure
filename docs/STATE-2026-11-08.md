## What was measured, and against what

**The parity rounds (Stage 2).** Five 50-turn rounds on Gemma, every role, stakes live, an Opus player, read as a
player and paired blind against the D98 base rounds (noir e62980aae2a7, station e2a31eafbd29). Round 1 of each game
carried the split-line bug (B38) and mostly measured it. Round 2 is the clean comparison.

| | base | r22L | what the readers said |
|---|---|---|---|
| noir, huge successes / failures | 16 / 19 in 63 checks | 12 / 12 in 38, ended at T34 | late turns to r22L in both orders; the case bought on one roll |
| station, huge successes / failures | 16 / 12 in 51 | 41 / 5 in 61 | "escorted rather than played"; the sample can never be learned (B61) |
| retellings | 9 (noir) | 0 in every parity round | |
| late formula, late half | 0/25, 1/25 | 0/17, 0/25 | gone since D145's count |

Verdict: the base won overall in all four pairs, both orders agreeing; the parity rounds won or tied on writing and
continuity and lost on story escalation. The cause and the fix are D168 (the station one step harder by stats and
offset together, the -2 cap, B63's two rules). No long round has run on D168 yet; five smoke turns per game were the
check. The owner's standing rule holds: no decision on a lead without data per game, and the next rounds are the data.

**The fantasy's rounds.** f1L (Sonnet, 50 turns, Wren the tiefling rogue): would play on eagerly; five faults fixed in
D165. f2S (ten turns after the midday fixes): the opening at turn 0 and the race line land; renown still never moved
because the cellar job ended in a way the hook has no ending for (B68, content); coppers counted as gold (B69).

**The keyed sets** after the W1 pass: the noir referee's errors 11 to 6 at the same required count; the station's
required 31 to 33 of 48. The assistant's grade moved by one with no text change, so the replays are not fully
deterministic and B27's earlier drop is noise.
