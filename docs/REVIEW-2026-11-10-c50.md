## What the reader found, and what was done

### Correctness (fixed tonight)

1. **The days ran before the turn's kind was known.** The `clock_moved` hook (journal "Tuesday morning came.", the tally,
   THE CLOCK MOVED) ran before the assistant; a clarify or impossible reading then moved no clock, and the save recorded days
   that never ran. *Fixed:* the hook runs after `_resolve`, only when the clock moved, with what actually ran.
2. **B105 was broken for every game.** `quiet = trivial and not elapsed` let any trivial turn with minutes tick events.
   *Fixed:* only a lead ticks a trivial turn.
3. **The split hid the lead.** With stakes live the narrator read "the first attempt starts about 960 minutes on" and nothing
   of the jump. *Fixed:* THIS TURN names the lead first, and the split's line after it.
4. **A sleep or wait to the named time overshot it** (the turn's own minutes floored on top). *Fixed:* a wait or sleep with
   a lead takes no minutes of its own.
5. **Smaller:** the lead reported whole when the clock stopped short (now what ran, with `lead_short`); a stop during the
   turn's own minutes dropped the "not finished yet" line (now kept); "quarter to one" was 00:45; "at eight" at 21:00 read
   as nothing (now tomorrow's eight); "8 p.m." lost its p.m. (the boundary). All fixed, each with a test.
6. **False runs of the clock.** Every sentence start was an anchor, so "I ask about the show. Tomorrow she has a lesson."
   ran a day; "until Sunday" read as Sunday 09:00 while the Skip means Sunday 22:00, and the Skip lever was then dropped.
   *Fixed:* a time counts only where a sentence opens on it and the player acts in it ("…, I", "…, we"); "until" is no
   anchor (a wait to a time is the Skip's and the assistant's, as before).

### Second homes (rule 2)

7. **The days-ran rule was worded twice** (the Skip directive and THE CLOCK MOVED). *Fixed:* one `_days_ran` in the clown's
   game, both directives call it.
8. **The assistant's "time_minutes is the task's own from there" was in two homes** (the pipeline's per-turn note and the
   clown's standing rules), and the engine's note named "Skip", a clown lever. *Fixed:* the standing line removed; the
   engine's note no longer names a game's command.
9. **Open:** "now in absolute minutes" is computed in four places in `pipeline.py` (`named_lead`, `_resolve` twice,
   `_clock_moved`); one `_now_abs(game, doc)` would serve. Cheap; not done tonight (it touches every game's turn).
10. **Open, by design:** the engine's waking words (`UNTIL_MORNING` 07:00, night 19:00) and the clown's parts of the day
    (morning 09:00, night 21:00) differ. They mean different things (waking against the house's working day), but a third
    game reading times would face the choice; say so in the time doc when one does.

### Regex debt (rule 1)

11. **The time parser is a hand tokenizer** (an anchor, a clause reader, a clock pattern, a parts table, number words) on
    the player's own words. It is the place a list could grow: "midnight", "in two hours", "the day after tomorrow",
    "Tues", "Wednesday's show". **The field that could carry it:** the assistant's reading gaining `opens_at {day, part,
    hour}` (a closed table turns it into minutes; `_resolve` already takes `lead_minutes`), with the parser kept narrow as
    the shim and a cross-check that logs disagreements. Not done tonight: the staked reading is at the edge of Sonnet's
    strict-tool grammar (D202), so a new field needs a real Sonnet extract first, and the assistant would then read the
    turn before the clock moved. **The stop rule, written into the code:** the vocabulary is closed; a phrase it misses is a
    C-line, not a word added.

### Engine and game

12. `clock_stop` is a good extraction (the stops had one home in `_resolve`; now the lead and the turn share it).
    `named_lead`, `_clock_moved`, `clock_commands` are generic hooks; only the clown answers them. If a second game wants
    named times, the parser moves to the engine beside `until_when` (or the field above replaces it): never a copy.

## Tests (rule 3)

The parser has two or more phrasings for each shape (a weekday and a part, tomorrow with a clock, a later sentence, we as
well as I, the past, the near, the week's cap, the hour's sense both ways). The pipeline path: the named time through the
fake provider, the stop at a pending, and the faults above (the clarify that moves nothing, the trivial without a lead, the
sleep to the named time, THIS TURN with a split and stopped short). Not yet covered: a referee retell of a turn with a lead
(the lead is recomputed from the same words and state, so it should match), quoted speech (the pipeline strips quotes
before the hook).
